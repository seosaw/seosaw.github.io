    Science plan · 2025

    Updated SEOSAW Science Plan

    SEOSAW has a new Science Plan, replacing the 2018 inception plan. Developed by the SSC, it sets out the key scientific and policy questions to address over the next five years, spanning vegetation demographics, biodiversity, functional ecology, and policy and land management.

    Event · September 2025

    Zambia Permanent Vegetation Plots Workshop

    Around 20 participants from Zambia and southern Africa gathered at Copperbelt University (CBU) to review the status of permanent sample plots for long-term vegetation monitoring. The workshop was co-led by Prof. Sally Archibald (WITS) and Prof. Stephen Syampungani (CBU), and focused on strengthening Zambia's PSP network, positioning CBU as a hub for SEOSAW activities, and improving data use and regional collaboration. Colleagues from CIFOR and the Zambian Forestry Department noted the initiative's value for national policy.

    Fieldwork · 2025

    15 years of PSP data in Kilwa, Tanzania

    From September to December 2025, 21 permanent sample plots in Kilwa, Tanzania were re-censused — the 5th census for 13 plots and the 6th for 5 others, extending continuous data collection to 15 years. The campaign was led by PhD students Thomas Malima (Sokoine University of Agriculture) and Oliver McFarlane (University of Edinburgh), in collaboration with long-time SEOSAW partners Mpingo Conservation and Development Initiative (MCDI).

    Fieldwork · 2025

    New plots in Zimbabwe's Baikiaea woodlands

    Praxedes (Vimbai) Rukuni (WITS, supervised by Dr Justice Muvengwi) has established 8 new 1 ha permanent sample plots in Zimbabwe's Zambezi teak (Baikiaea plurijuga) woodlands — 5 in gazetted forests (Gwampa and Gwaai state forests) and 3 in communal lands (Nkayi and Lupane). Most SEOSAW PSPs are in relatively protected landscapes, making these plots in lived-in landscapes a valuable addition to the network's coverage.

    Project · 2025

    Bio-Bridge project — coastal forests of Kenya and Tanzania

    Led by Dr Itambo Malombe (National Museums of Kenya) and funded by the UN Convention on Biological Diversity's Bio-Bridge Initiative, this project is strengthening community capacity to conserve threatened species in eastern coastal forest biodiversity hotspots. Since June 2025, the project has established 2 new SEOSAW PSPs in coastal forests of Kenya and Tanzania — data from which have been used to conclude that these forests meet IUCN Key Biodiversity Area criteria. Two community workshops were held covering indigenous knowledge and PSP data collection.
